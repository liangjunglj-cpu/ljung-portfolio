S1 · −12°C

THE CROSSING

Open water, overcast arctic daylight, glare off water. Engine, hull slap, wind — no music, ever. The machine's vapor plume is the first landmark. The coldest the visitor will ever be.

S2 · +2M

THE GATEWAY

Hard shadow stripes through the crown lattice; one warm lamp over the bylaws plate only. Bare fingertip required for the release — one deliberate discomfort. Your memory visualizes once, sinks through the glass floor, and is gone.

S3 · 0 → −16M

THE DESCENT

Daylight dies in the first four meters. Below the waterline, only joint-mounted ice caps glow faint blue at each 4.4 m pitch — a countable rhythm. Your own breathing appears.

S4 · −24M

THE FAMILY WING

Each memory is its own small luminaire; a room's brightness IS its crowding. Dense rooms glow, thin rooms are dark frame. Doors exist only between kin — the receipt's three names are the wayfinding.

S5 · +2°C

THE GAP ROOM

Each exhibited memory is lit twice — once warm from its declared stance, once cold from where measurement placed it. The two shadows do not agree. The one hands-off room.

S6 · +18°C

THE COMMONS

Warm 3000K everywhere — the machine's rejected heat given as light. Voices, steam, kettle. The first warm surface since the crossing is the rail at the threshold, placed deliberately.

P.05

WORKING RULES

EXPERIMENTS IN GOVERNANCE14 NUMBERS PER MEMORY
THE MEASUREMENT

Each reading in a splat is exactly fourteen numbers — position, size, color, opacity, lean — standardized so a meter of position counts no more than a shade of color. Two memories are kin when they sit close in that fourteen-dimensional space; UMAP squashes it to three, preserving neighborhoods, never distances. Only adjacency is trustworthy.

THE CROWDING RULE

Where memories crowd, walls thicken; where they thin, the building stays open. Six readings make an enclosed archive room, three make a lighter research room, thin spots stay open frame. Each family's top cell becomes its commons — the warm room.

THE KIT

Rods of one diameter in four lengths, one universal joint that accepts any angle, panels of one size, one wedge clip that fastens every seam. Program lives in the panels, not the frame — rearranging the museum means clipping and unclipping, never demolition.

THE SEASON

Once every freeze season the archive recounts its families and the building rearranges to match. The bylaws are reprinted; the old printing is deposited into the archive as a memory of the museum itself. No room is ever promised twice — but the entrance and exit never move.

THE ONE-SENTENCE VERSION — people give their memories of one place; mathematics only counts the resemblances; architecture turns the counting into rooms, corridors, warmth and ice; and every winter the whole thing is honestly rebuilt to match what people now remember.
